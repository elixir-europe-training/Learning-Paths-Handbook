## What is this step about?
Once the DOMAIN and TARGET AUDIENCE have been identified, this step begins the iterative cycle of the protocol.
During this step, all knowledge AREAS to be addressed throughout the Learning Path are identified.
The structure of knowledge AREAS and TOPICS (defined in the following step) guides how content is organised and delivered.

## What are AREA(S)

!!! info "An AREA is ..."

    In this context, an AREA refers to a broad group of knowledge that includes related topic(s) (more granular and detailed units of knowledge) (step 4) to be covered during the learning path. Areas, such as domains (step 1) and topics (step 3), have no clearly defined borders between each other [EDAM](https://edamontology.github.io/edam-browser/#topic_0003).

## Why do we need to identify AREA(S)?
- Knowledge AREAS structure the Learning Path logically and systematically, serving as an intermediate level of organisation between the DOMAIN and specific TOPICS.
- They provide a clear roadmap for educators and learners, enhancing the learning experience.
- A structured approach supports a comprehensive educational experience that covers the necessary competencies.
- The approach gives Learning Path designers (domain experts) the flexibility to select TOPICS (step 4) that best fit each AREA.


## How do we identify AREA(S)?
- this protocol wants to be a user-friendly and flexible protocol that can be adapted to various contexts for implementing LPs (TeSS, academic context, institutions, etc.); when implementing a LP in a platform like TeSS, it could be useful to leverage existing keywords and vocabulary (to ensure consistency and discoverability)
- for each learning path (LP), we start with a manageable number of knowledge Areas (5-6 maximum) or (to go beyond this number) Areas closely connected to the overall subject to facilitate the structuring of the content
- ensure that the Areas cover competencies and skills to be includes into the LP
- ensure that the Areas are neither too broad or too narrow, and adjust the number of areas if necessary
- review and refine the knowledge Areas based on the topics identified in the following step
- conduct a final review and revision of the knowledge Areas at the end of the protocol cycle
